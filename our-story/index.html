For Families For Businesses

Our Story

I lived both sides of the problem.
Now I am building the connection.

Nancy Hassan
Founder of Save4Good | Woman founder and mom of three

The moment I could not forget

I am a mom of three. I know that taking care of a family means making a hundred decisions that no one else sees. What can we afford this week? What do the kids need? How do we make time for everything when the cost of everyday life keeps climbing?

After a long day at work, I bought the baby formula my daughter needed for $70. The following day, while grocery shopping, I saw the exact same formula tucked away on a final clearance shelf for just $10. I did not buy it. I had already purchased ours.

But I could not forget it. I kept thinking about the parents who might have needed that exact product and never known it was there. It was already available. Somewhere, a family was still looking.

Why should finding something your family genuinely needs depend on being in the right aisle at exactly the right moment?

Then I saw how often good intentions miss real needs

While volunteering to distribute donated grocery boxes, I saw families open them and find things they could not use or foods they did not eat. I thought about community centers trying to stretch their budgets and senior homes with wishlists and dietary needs that rarely reach the businesses that might be able to help.

The generosity was real. So was the need. But the two did not always meet. People did not simply need more products. They needed the right products.

I also knew what businesses were carrying

Years of leading hospitality operations and managing people showed me the other side. I know what it takes to protect margins, control labor costs, keep an operation moving, and deliver for customers when there is never enough time in the day.

Business owners can see opportunities in unsold products, supplies, and meals. But acting on them often means another system, another task, another person taking pictures, tracking inventory, coordinating handoffs, and documenting results. I knew that asking busy teams to do more was not the answer.

I would think about the hot meals prepared in grocery stores and the people working long shifts in nearby warehouses who might love a warm lunch but cannot leave work to get one. There was value on one side and demand on the other. The connection was missing.

That is why I founded Save4Good

I wanted to build the infrastructure that brings those worlds closer together. A way for businesses to see demand, reach new buyers, recover more value, and make stronger decisions. A way for families to find what they truly need, and for organizations to connect help with real, visible needs.

The vision reaches across grocery stores, restaurants, hotels, pharmacies, apparel, employers, and communities. Each has different challenges. All can benefit from a better connection between what is available and what is needed.

I want the opportunity to reach other women, too

I am building Save4Good as a woman and a mom of three, and I want that to mean something beyond my own story. As the company grows, I hope it creates meaningful opportunities for other women and mothers to contribute, build skills, earn, and grow with us.

I know how much women carry for their families. I also know how much talent and ambition can go unseen when opportunity is hard to reach. If Save4Good can help open even more of those doors, that will be part of the value we create.

More revenue for businesses. More possibilities for people. More good for our planet.
Nancy Hassan, Founder of Save4Good
