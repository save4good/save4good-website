For Families For Businesses

Our Story

I saw the problem from both sides.
So I decided to build the connection.

Nancy Hassan, Founder of Save4Good. Woman-founded. Inspired by a mom of three.

Built by a Mom of Three. Shaped by Years Behind the Scenes.

As a mother of three, I know how much thought goes into taking care of a family. Grocery shopping is not simply another errand. It is making sure everyone has what they need, managing a budget, and finding ways to make everything work when the cost of living keeps rising.

One evening, after a long day at work, I purchased my daughter's baby formula for $70. The following day, while grocery shopping, I happened to notice the exact same formula tucked away in a final-clearance aisle for just $10. I did not buy it. I had already purchased what my daughter needed. But I kept thinking about it. Somewhere, another parent might have been looking for that exact product without knowing it was available on clearance. Why should discovering something you genuinely need depend on being in the right aisle at the right moment? The insight is visibility into already available merchant-controlled offers, not undercutting or cross-store price competition.

While volunteering with a community organization, I helped distribute grocery boxes donated to families in need. I watched some families open their boxes and find products they could not use or foods they did not eat. The businesses were trying to help. The organizations were doing their best. The families needed support. But something was missing. The donations were not always connected to the actual needs of the people receiving them.

I thought about senior homes with shopping lists, dietary restrictions, allergies, and specific needs that businesses may never see. And community organizations trying to stretch their budgets further while supporting more families. People do not just need access to more products. They need access to the right products.

Then there was the side of the story I knew from my career. After years of leading operations, managing teams, and working in hospitality, I understand the pressures businesses face. I have seen how hard managers work to protect their margins, control labor costs, manage inventory, and deliver a good customer experience. I have also seen how easily potential revenue can be lost when a business simply does not have the time or resources to pursue every opportunity. Because recovering inventory is not as simple as deciding to sell it. Someone has to identify what is available, manage the information, update pricing, coordinate transactions, and maintain records of what was sold, donated, or discarded. And when businesses are already short on time and staff, asking employees to scan more items, take more pictures, and manage another system can become another burden. Business owners do not necessarily need another task. They need a smarter way to turn the work they are already doing into more opportunity.

I kept thinking about grocery stores preparing fresh, hot meals throughout the day. Some of those meals may go unsold. Meanwhile, someone working a long shift at a nearby warehouse would appreciate a convenient, affordable hot meal but may not have the time to leave work to get one. The grocery store has something valuable to offer. The employee has a real need. Both could benefit from that connection. And yet, the opportunity can be missed.

The more I looked, the clearer it became. Families have shopping lists, budgets, and everyday essentials they cannot go without. Employers have employees who need convenient meals. Community organizations and senior homes have specific purchasing needs. Grocery stores have inventory, fresh meals, and products that could reach more customers. Restaurants have prepared food, changing demand, and opportunities to generate additional sales. Hotels manage food, amenities, supplies, and other valuable resources while balancing labor costs and demanding operations. Pharmacies serve families looking for affordable health and household essentials, with opportunities to improve the visibility and availability of eligible products. Each business operates differently. Each faces its own challenges. But they share something important. They have value to offer, customers to reach, and opportunities that can be difficult to capture with the time and resources available. The demand exists. The value exists. The missing piece is the infrastructure connecting them.

That is why I founded Save4Good. I wanted to build one connected infrastructure that supports businesses and the people they serve. A place where grocery stores, hotels, restaurants, pharmacies, and other businesses can better understand demand, reach new customers, generate additional revenue, make smarter inventory decisions, and simplify the work behind it all. Where families can discover products they genuinely need, at prices that make everyday life a little easier. Where employees can connect with convenient meal options, and businesses can turn existing kitchen capacity into new sales opportunities. Where community organizations and senior homes can make their real needs visible, so purchasing and donations are guided by what people actually need. And where businesses remain in control of their inventory, prices, and decisions, while gaining access to new opportunities. Because businesses should not have to choose between growing their revenue and doing something good for their communities.

From One Mom's Experience to Something Much Bigger.

Save4Good was born from my experience as a mother, shaped by my years in business operations, and driven by the belief that we can do better for both sides. And as it grows, I hope it creates meaningful opportunities for other women and mothers to grow alongside it, without named jobs, a program, or a hiring promise.

I believe that when businesses become stronger, they can create more opportunities for the people and communities around them. When families have better access to what they need, everyday life becomes a little easier. And when we make better use of the resources we already have, our planet benefits too.

More revenue for businesses. More possibilities for people. More good for our planet.

Woman-founded. Inspired by a mom of three. Built to connect what matters.

There are three interconnected economic benefits: For businesses, local support with customer discovery, onboarding, marketplace operations and relationships. For families, better visibility into affordable goods, meals and community resources. For mothers, potential access to flexible paid work, professional training and career development.
